Stylometric patterns and their implications

  1. Repeated narrative machinery. All four episodes relocate the origin of an internationally familiar musical sound to an obscure Romanian encounter. They repeatedly convert a local noise, body, or setting into an explanation of a musician’s later work. This regularity is compatible with repeated expansion of a prompt, but also with a deliberately constrained literary sequence. It is a hypothesis about composition, not a validated AI marker.
  2. A clear orthographic seam. The first three episodes have 57 tokens containing internal î and three containing â. The Kraftwerk episode has zero internal-î tokens and 14 internal-â tokens. Comparable forms include cînd versus când, and fîș versus fâș. This suggests inconsistent normalization or separately handled material. Publication editing, copying, or human revision could produce the same pattern. Some â spellings in the first three episodes are a proper name or a quoted song title.
  3. Explicit explanatory endings. În realitate occurs twice; the Billie episode uses De asta and Era exact. The narration often explains the symbolic connection after dramatizing it. This may resemble model-generated explanatory closure, but it also carries the joke and the sequence’s aesthetic premise.
  4. Local detail and sensory accumulation. Geography, industrial brands, clothes, food, and bodily sensations give the text a consistent regional texture. Examples include Singer și Ileana, țigări Assos, and grea, densă și apoasă. These features can occur in a human author’s writing or in a detailed prompt and its output. Specificity does not independently establish human authorship.
  5. Sentence rhythm is variable. The sentence-length coefficient of variation is 0.549, with a mean of 24.1 and a standard deviation of 13.25 word tokens. There are six sentences of at most five words and one of 68 words. No three-word sentence opening repeats exactly. The evidence therefore does not support a strong claim of monotonous sentence rhythm in this text.

The spelling seam and the fully AI-labeled Kraftwerk episode coincide. This is a useful localization of the question, not evidence that the detector uses spelling as its decision rule. In the first three episodes, 56.3% of word units also fall in AI-labeled spans of this same scan.

What this says about the claim

The disclosed collaboration is plausible and receives a positive detector signal. The repeated episode structure and the final episode’s spelling discontinuity are concrete patterns worth investigating. They do not establish that Gemini generated the whole piece, or identify which passages Sociu wrote or edited. Human-labeled spans are not authenticated human writing, and AI-labeled spans are not generation logs.

Actual drafts or generation records would provide stronger evidence. A separate attribution study would require authenticated Sociu prose and matched Romanian fiction from candidate models, controlling for genre, topic, length, and spelling. No likelihood ratio, authorship probability, or author-distance score is justified by this single text.
